## The company

Northwind Analytics sells a managed data-pipeline platform — connectors, transformation and
warehouse loading — to mid-market and enterprise data teams. Roughly 1,800 customers.

Founded 2017 by two former infrastructure engineers. Raised a Series D in late 2025. Their buyer
is typically the Head of Data or VP Engineering, and they compete against a mix of open-source
tooling and one large incumbent.

## Market context

The data-integration market has consolidated significantly since 2023. The pressure is on
managed platforms to justify their price against increasingly capable open-source alternatives,
which pushes vendors toward reliability and support as differentiators rather than features.

Their own infrastructure costs are therefore a competitive input, not just a cost line — if
their margins compress, their pricing gets harder.

## Competitive position

| | Northwind | Large incumbent | Open source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connectors | Broad | Broadest | Fragmented |
| Reliability | Improving | Strong | Variable |
| Price | Mid | High | Low / self-hosted |

They win on breadth-per-dollar and lose on enterprise trust. Reliability has been their soft
spot, which is why the June database migration mattered more commercially than technically.

## The relationship

Started 2024 on standard enterprise terms after graduating from startup credits. The relationship
was transactional for the first year and became genuinely collaborative after the reliability
work began in early 2026.

The turning point was the managed database migration (2026-06-19), which removed their
longest-standing operational complaint.

Their stated priorities for the coming year: cost predictability, reliability, and preparing the
business for a public offering signalled for 2027.
