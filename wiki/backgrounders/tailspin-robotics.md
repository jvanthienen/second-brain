## The company

Tailspin Robotics builds warehouse automation — autonomous mobile robots plus the fleet software
that coordinates them. Around 200 employees, Series C, growing quickly in logistics and
third-party fulfilment.

Their differentiator is a vision model that handles unstructured environments, which is also the
source of their infrastructure profile: they retrain per deployment site, so compute cost scales
with the number of robots in the field rather than with revenue.

## Market context

Warehouse automation is in a land-grab phase. Several well-funded competitors, customers running
pilots across multiple vendors, and contract wins that are large and lumpy.

This shapes their commercial behaviour more than anything else: they cannot forecast eighteen
months out with confidence, because a single logistics contract can change their compute
requirement by 40%.

## Competitive position

Strong technically, thin commercially. They win pilots on vision performance and lose some
enterprise deals on support maturity.

## The relationship

Two years, entirely pay-as-you-go. The technical relationship is deep — [[Kai Nakamura]]'s team
works closely with ours and prefers our tooling.

The commercial relationship has never developed past transacting. We have no executive coverage,
no commitment, and until August 2026 no competitive pressure — which in retrospect meant nobody
felt urgency to fix any of that.
