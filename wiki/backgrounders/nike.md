## The company

Nike is a global sportswear brand: footwear, apparel and equipment sold through direct
channels and wholesale partners in most major markets.

The direct-to-consumer business is the strategic centre of the company, and the consumer-data
analytics platform behind it runs on us: membership, personalization and demand signals across
app, web and the retail estate. Inside Nike the platform sits with engineering under
[[Sofia Marchetti]], with [[Daniel Osei]]'s data platform team operating it day to day.

## Market context

Global sportswear is mature and fiercely contested, and growth increasingly comes from digital
and direct channels rather than wholesale. That puts the consumer-data platform at the centre
of the growth story at exactly the moment investors are pressing on margins: hence the
board-level cost transformation the CFO is running.

Their infrastructure costs are therefore inside the transformation's scope, not outside it:
the analytics platform is both a growth engine and a reviewable line item.

## Competitive position

| | Nike | Heritage rivals | Digital-native challengers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand reach | Global | Global | Narrow |
| Direct-to-consumer | Strong, growing | Lagging | Native |
| Price point | Premium | Premium | Mid |

They win on brand and lose ground when the digital experience lags. Reliability of the
analytics platform has been the soft spot, which is why the June migration mattered more
commercially than technically.

## The relationship

Started 2024 on standard enterprise terms after an initial analytics workload landed well. The
relationship was transactional for the first year and became genuinely collaborative after the
reliability work began in early 2026.

The turning point was the consumer-data analytics platform migration (2026-06-19), which removed
their longest-standing operational complaint.

Their stated priorities for the coming year: cost predictability, reliability, and the
board-level cost transformation now under way.
