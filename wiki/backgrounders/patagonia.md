## The company

Patagonia is an outdoor apparel brand: technical clothing and gear sold direct through
e-commerce and its own stores, with a smaller wholesale business. Growing quickly, with the
growth concentrated in the direct digital channel.

Their differentiator on our side of the house is how seriously they invest in ML:
demand-forecasting and recommendation models trained on GPUs, plus the e-commerce data platform
underneath. Models retrain as the catalogue and the season turn over, so compute cost scales
with model-training volume rather than with revenue.

## Market context

Outdoor apparel is seasonal and hit-driven. Demand concentrates around peak season, and a
strong or soft peak changes the shape of the whole year.

This shapes their commercial behaviour more than anything else: they cannot forecast eighteen
months out with confidence, because a single peak season can change their compute requirement
by 40%.

## Competitive position

Strong brand, crowded category. They win customers on product quality and the digital
experience, and lose share when larger competitors outspend them on reach.

## The relationship

Two years, entirely pay-as-you-go. The technical relationship is deep — [[Kai Nakamura]]'s team
works closely with ours and prefers our tooling.

The commercial relationship has never developed past transacting. We have no executive coverage,
no commitment, and until August 2026 no competitive pressure — which in retrospect meant nobody
felt urgency to fix any of that.
