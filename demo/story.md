# The story: who is who in the zoo

You are not demoing software. You are telling a story that happens to run on software. This
file is the plot, the cast, and the tension, so you can narrate instead of describe.

**The premise:** you play yourself. For tonight, Josefina is an enterprise seller at Azura
Cloud (a fictional provider) carrying an $8.5M target across three accounts: Nike, Patagonia,
Nordstrom. The accounts and people are invented; the seller is real.
It is Thursday. The most important meeting of your quarter is next Thursday. An hour ago,
one off-the-record sentence about one person came out of your prep call with Sofia.

**One story: Daniel.** After the co-host review (2026-08-11) the demo follows a single
character. Daniel Osei is introduced in the slides at the very start and reinforced on every
slide; the demo pays him off. Everything else in this file is still true and still loaded in
the repo, but on stage it is set dressing. Narrate Daniel, let the rest happen on screen.

## The tension (say this out loud, in your own words)

**Beat 1, the sentence:** your prep call with Sofia is barely over and its note is already
in the brain's inbox. It reads like any meeting note. Except one line in the middle, off the
record: Daniel is interviewing elsewhere. The person the room already met in your slides.
The person your renewal quietly runs through.

**Beat 2, the memory:** the brain ingests the note and does three things with that one
sentence. It writes the flight risk onto Daniel's page, marked off the record. It recognizes
the fact as an instance of a risk pattern it already knew: too much of this account runs
through one person. And it infers, from months of meetings he appears in, how Daniel
actually decides: evidence over enthusiasm. Nobody typed any of that. A dozen other pages
update around it; you do not chase them.

**Beat 3, the corner:** the hood comes off, but only Daniel's corner of it: his page, his
links, his risk pattern. Markdown files on your laptop, written by the agent, never by you.
The full galaxy stays in the slides.

**Beat 4, the turn:** intel becomes judgment, twice. The brief assembles from pages that
changed three minutes ago: bring Daniel evidence, not enthusiasm; flag the continuity risk
to the team WITHOUT the off-the-record detail. And the one-page backgrounder rides along as
a PDF: who is in the room, the goals, the key points, and what we just learned, numbered.
You read both, you approve, the brief lands in the team channel and you attach the PDF with
your own hands. Nothing auto-sends, and the one thing that should stay private stayed
private.

**The clock:** the call was an hour ago. The QBR that decides a $12M renewal is next
Thursday. The demo is that hour, played at real speed.
